The Knowledge Network

A cross-sector consortium of funders, AI labs, workforce development leaders, and researchers, built out of a convening series and launched with Anthropic.

Convenings produce goodwill that evaporates. The Knowledge Network was an attempt to catch that goodwill before it did.

The problem

Over two quarters I ran eight curated executive convenings, bringing together more than 200 leaders from philanthropy, AI labs, workforce development, and research. The conversations were good. The pattern afterward was familiar: a strong room, a few follow-ups, and then everyone returns to their own institution and the shared thinking dissolves.

Meanwhile the underlying problem persisted. Everyone in those rooms was running some version of the same experiment on AI and workforce outcomes, and almost none of it was being shared. Organizations were paying separately to learn the same lessons.

What I did

I converted the convening base into a standing consortium with a reason to exist between meetings: open-sourcing what members learn about AI's real impact on students and workers.

  • Defined the membership model and the value exchange, so that joining meant contributing rather than only attending.
  • Recruited founding members out of the existing convening relationships instead of starting cold.
  • Launched it with Anthropic, which gave the consortium credibility with both funders and practitioners.

Founding membership ran well past what we planned for, which is the most useful signal about how badly the field wanted somewhere to compare notes.

Where it landed

The Network is live and gathering as a full group. Its output is public by design: what members learn about deployment, adoption, and outcomes gets released rather than held.

Members include Anthropic, Microsoft AI, the Stanford Accelerator for Learning, Per Scholas, CodePath, and Opportunity@Work, among others.
