The portfolio

The mission of the portfolio was direct services and policies that focus on building strength and resilience, including access to quality health services, safe spaces, education, economic advancement, and leadership development.

I directed and evaluated grant investments across climate, disaster relief, health, gender equity, and education, managing the full grant lifecycle including budget oversight and impact measurement. I cultivated more than 80 funder and nonprofit partner relationships, expanding program reach by 30%.

I managed five assessors, each a specialist in one of the issue areas I funded, so that expert partnership and collaboration continued once the funds were distributed.

I executed more than 30 convenings and public-sector engagements, including a UN summit and a US Congressional briefing. I was also lead producer on more than 10 documentary-style short films for the NBC fundraisers.

Hand in Hand

The $62M Hand in Hand Hurricane Relief Fund was raised in the fall of 2017. I was brought on to lead it: identify the grantees, distribute the funds over two years, and assess the programs on the ground across Texas, Florida, and Puerto Rico. The fund closed in 2022 with all funds spent.

Giving the decision away

The part of this work I am most attached to is the smallest by dollar value. I piloted participatory grantmaking at Comic Relief US and founded the Youth Advisory Council to put young people in the room where grant decisions actually get made.

The inaugural council was eight young activists from six countries: a filmmaker in Los Angeles, a Colombian political scientist, a grants officer whose family fled the genocide in Bosnia and Herzegovina, a documentary photographer working in Somalia and Kenya, a scholarship officer in Cambodia, a member of the Sac and Fox Tribe of Oklahoma studying mental health advocacy, and two students working on education equity and girls' empowerment.

They did the real work. The council developed the proposal criteria, vetted 175 grantee applicants, and selected the first cohort themselves. That became the Juntanza Fund, named from a practice in Afro-descendant communities in Colombia meaning a union to help one another achieve a common goal.

When you create a safe, supported and informed space for young people, it's incredible how strong their voices grow.

My comment in LA Downtown News on the first Juntanza Fund cohort, January 2022

The first grantees were a community farming organization in Kenya, an anti-racist education project in the US, a mental health service for Afro-descendant communities and migrants in Colombia, a women-led coalition supporting survivors of gender-based violence in Somalia, and a girls' education and career readiness program in rural Cambodia.

Where it landed

Both the Youth Advisory Council and the Juntanza Fund are still running, four years past my tenure. They were designed to survive the person who started them, which is the only test of this kind of work that I find convincing.
