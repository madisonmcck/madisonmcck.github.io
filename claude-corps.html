Claude Corps

Claude Corps had to solve two different problems at once, for two audiences who rarely appear in the same program design.

The problem

Early-career people are entering a labor market that is changing underneath them. At the same time, nonprofits are being told that AI will transform their operations, without the staff capacity to find out whether that is true. Both groups need the same thing from opposite directions: someone who can actually do the work.

What made the design hard is that AI capacity does not mean one thing across the nonprofit sector. It looks different by region, by area of service, and by the size of the organization. A program that assumed a single template would have failed most of its host sites.

What I did

I co-created the program with Anthropic: co-designed the model, secured executive buy-in on both sides, and drove it from concept to national launch in nine weeks. That meant making the partnership case internally, shaping the fellowship structure, and getting host organizations to commit before the program had a track record to point at.

  • Designed the fellowship model to serve fellows and host organizations as two distinct user groups with different success measures.
  • Built the host organization pipeline and the recruitment approach for the first cohort.
  • Positioned the program publicly, including the launch announcement with Anthropic and the fireside at the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation.

AI transformation for a nonprofit is an operations question. What do we change about how we run so cost per outcome comes down and we can serve more people, while quality holds or improves?

From my remarks at the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation Leadership Circle Summit

That framing came from evidence rather than theory. At CodePath, our own AI integrations drove cost per student down while job outcomes held. The Corps is an attempt to make that available to organizations that cannot build it themselves.

Where it landed

The program launched nationally with Anthropic. Applications for the inaugural cohort closed in July, and those first 100 fellows start in October 2026, with at least 400 nonprofits hosting over the twelve months that follow. Demand from host organizations ran well ahead of what we planned for, which is itself a signal about how much unmet need sits in the sector.
