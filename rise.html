The Rise Fellowship

A global fellowship for young people aged 15 to 17, expanding educational and entrepreneurial pathways.

400global fellows selected
10,000community members
~1,000documented life changes in three years
95%sustained engagement

The unique edge for Rise was that we had to select talent from any country and a range of socioeconomic backgrounds, and then design a program that would be catalytic for them for their lifetime.

The problem

Finding brilliant 15 to 17 year olds anywhere in the world is a solvable problem. Keeping them engaged through the years when their lives change fastest, and converting a selection into an actual change in trajectory, is the harder one. A fellowship that goes quiet after the announcement has spent its money on a press release. My work focused on the participatory design, the programs to support non-traditional and traditional pathways, a venture fund for their innovations paired with learning and mentorship, holistic support resources and emergency funds, and community design to create a truly lifelong fellowship and network.

What I did

I designed and led the program end to end after fellows were selected: programming, community, communications, and the follow-on funding that lets fellows act on what they learn.

  • Built 25 interdisciplinary, experiential learning programs for the global fellowship network, sustaining 95% engagement.
  • Designed and executed three global summits with more than 150 industry experts in South Africa and the U.K.
  • Ran two Discord communities totaling more than 10,000 members, and produced a monthly newsletter reaching 600,000 readers, growing engagement 50% year over year.
  • Secured more than 40 follow-on funding commitments for fellows' work.

In three years we reported roughly 1,000 significant life changes: job placements, acceptances to top universities, scholarships, and major career advancements.

Rise program outcomes, 2022 to 2025

What the fellows built

The 2024 Global Winners cohort gives a sense of the range: a non-disruptive fishing net material for euphotic zones, an automated street cleaning robot aimed at reducing mosquito-breeding refuse, a 3D-printed mind-controlled prosthetic arm that requires no brain surgery, and a plasma fertilizer that reduces crop loss by enriching irrigation water. That year brought the program's first winners from Bolivia, Czechia, Ecuador, Ethiopia, Kyrgyzstan, Nepal, Niger, Panama, Puerto Rico, Rwanda, Serbia, Sudan, and Syria.

Early signals of what the program compounds into are starting to show. Onkar Singh, one of the youngest founders to go through Y Combinator, founded Apolink, a satellite communications company that has raised $4.3M.

Foundation to compound for lifetimes

Rise continues past my tenure. The community, the summit format, and the follow-on funding structure continue for the fourth and final cohort as they pursue their academic and professional careers.
